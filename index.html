Research review · September 2026

Topic-based navigation for podcast archives

Sixteen hours of recorded interviews hold about as much argument as a book, with almost no way to search or cite them. This document reviews how other systems handle that problem, where each one stops, and the design we arrived at.

Thesis. Indexing recorded interviews by topic has always needed a person to listen and annotate. Most of that work can now be automated, while keeping the two things that made manual indexing worth doing: the publisher owns the index, and a person decides what goes into it.

01 · Access limitations

Access limitations in recorded scholarship

Written scholarship is addressable: located by section, cited by page, returned to by reference. Spoken scholarship has none of this. The atomic unit exposed to a listener is the episode, and the only navigation primitive is the seek bar.

LIMITATION 01

Retrieval

A listener who recalls a discussion of caste privilege in Western academia cannot determine which of sixteen episodes contains it, nor where inside it. Locating a ten minute passage approaches the cost of replaying the corpus.

LIMITATION 02

Citation

Audio resists precise citation, which depresses its use in subsequent work. Digital humanities scholarship identifies the absence of tooling for research and writing in audio as a structural constraint on the form.

LIMITATION 03

Accumulation

A theme raised in episode two and extended in episode eight is connected only in the memory of whoever heard both. The corpus does not become more useful as it grows. It becomes longer.

The historic bottleneck was transcription cost, and that has dissolved. The remaining bottleneck is structural: a transcript is a linearisation of audio, not an index of it. Converting eight thousand words of unpunctuated speech into an addressable structure requires judgments about topic boundary, salience and relation. These are interpretive tasks rather than transcription tasks.

02 · Prior art

Existing approaches

Each addresses part of the problem. None combines the required capabilities.

Archival indexing

OHMS · Univ. of Kentucky · 2014

Curator uploads a transcript, time synchronises it, and builds a time coded index. Delivers word level search connected to the exact moment in the recording. Exports XML into the institution's own system. Segment level citation is archival prior art rather than a recent development.

Owned and correctable.
Linear human cost.

Platform automation

Apple · Spotify · YouTube

Automatic transcripts and chapters at zero marginal cost to the publisher. Apple derives chapters from transcripts, labels them, and permits editing. Coverage has been documented as uneven, and chapters are intra episode by construction.

Free substrate.
No publisher owned output.

Consumer AI players

Snipd · Airr · Fathom

Transcription, automatic chaptering, natural language search inside episodes, and highlight capture that exports to personal knowledge tools. Structure accrues to the individual listener. A second listener starts from zero.

Optimised for consumption,
not curation.

Podcast knowledge graphs

Dexa

The closest prior art. Combines several indexing techniques with a knowledge graph mapping people, episodes and shows, resolving context and synonymy rather than exact match. Validates both the premise and the mechanism: a graph over podcast content is not a novel proposal.

Graphs entities.
Does not graph ideas.

The operative distinction is the choice of vertex. A graph of people and shows answers who said what about X. A graph of topics answers what positions does this corpus contain on X, and how do they relate, which is the question a scholarly interview series is designed to address.

03 · Trade-off analysis

Cost and ownership trade-off

Archival tools provide ownership and correctability at high human cost. Consumer AI applications provide automation but retain the structure and expose no correction path. No surveyed system provides both.

05 · Architecture

Pipeline architecture

Each stage writes an inspectable artefact to disk. This makes the pipeline auditable and gives the review stage a concrete object to check.

CONSTRAINT 01

Existing graph supplied as context

The ingesting agent receives every existing topic before it acts. Without this, each episode grows a parallel vocabulary and casteism and caste discrimination become separate vertices, halving the evidence on each.

CONSTRAINT 02

Closed relation vocabulary

Four labels only. Relation extraction is consistently the weaker operation in the literature, so the task is reduced from open generation to classification over a fixed vocabulary.

CONSTRAINT 03

Idempotent writes

Segments key on episode and start time. Re ingestion converges rather than duplicating, which is what makes correction and reprocessing safe to attempt.

07 · Error and review

Error sources and human review

Transcription error

Speech recognition approaches human accuracy on clean general domain English and degrades systematically on proper nouns, specialist terminology and non native accents. One specialist evaluation on dense proper noun content reported roughly four times the general domain error rate.

This corpus sits in the degraded regime: scholar names, Sanskrit and Punjabi terminology, institutional references, remote audio, and speakers across Canadian and Indian English. Assuming benchmark accuracy here would be unsound.

Error propagation

Entity extraction benchmarks above 0.95 F1 on expert annotated written text. Relation extraction is materially weaker. Neither figure accounts for transcription error entering upstream.

A misheard term produces a wrong topic, which attracts wrong evidence, which produces wrong edges. The validation literature is consistent that language models complement human validation rather than replace it.

The material is scholars' positions on caste, race and marginalisation, attributed by name, on a public academic site. An extraction error misattributes a political and professional position to a named academic. The acceptable error rate for unreviewed publication is therefore close to zero, independent of benchmark performance.

Review must nonetheless cost less than authoring, or the system offers no advantage over curatorial indexing. Three properties make that true: the reviewer checks a proposal rather than a transcript, every assertion carries a deep link to the audio that produced it, and corrections write straight to a graph the publisher owns.

08 · Limitations

Limitations

Recorded here because each is verifiable by a reviewer and material to the scope of the claim.

Where other systems perform better

Catalogue breadth. Dexa retrieves across many shows. This retrieves across one. For which podcast discussed X, Dexa wins outright.

Zero setup. Platform automation asks nothing of the publisher. This asks for a pipeline run and a review pass.

Direct answers. A conversational system synthesises a reply. This returns a map and its evidence, which is right for scholarship and wrong for a quick factual question.

Claims not yet substantiated

The review surface is designed, not built. The graph is editable today, but the purpose built ingestion review step does not exist. Until it does, human in the loop is architectural intent, not a demonstrated property.

One corpus. Sixteen episodes, one subject area, one interview format. Generalisation is unsupported without replication.

Speaker diarisation is not integrated. Attribution currently depends on structural cues rather than acoustic speaker identity.

09 · Evaluation

Evaluation plan

Six measures, specified in advance so the protocol is independent of the result.

1
Transcription accuracy on this corpus

Human transcribe six ten minute passages across both series. Report overall word error rate and proper noun error rate separately. The gap between them predicts downstream extraction error.

2
Timestamp fidelity

Sixty sampled boundaries. Median absolute offset under two seconds, ninety fifth percentile under five. Beyond five seconds a listener lands mid sentence and the citation loses credibility.

3
Extraction precision and recall

A domain reader independently indexes three episodes as a reference set. Topic precision below 0.80 defeats the economic argument, because review would then approach the cost of authoring.

4
Ontology stability

Ingest sequentially, recording topics created against topics reused. New vertex creation should decay as the graph matures. A flat curve indicates the reuse constraint is not binding.

5
Retrieval task performance

Twelve find the passage tasks, graph interface against transcript and scrub baseline, within subject and counterbalanced. No significant improvement defeats the central claim regardless of every other measure.

6
Review cost

Reviewer minutes per episode hour to reach publishable quality. Must run below roughly one third of the curatorial baseline to justify the pipeline over established archival practice.
